Key focus areas: Ratio calculations, risk interpretation, and working capital strategy.

Activity 1.1: Ratio Calculations & Gearing Interpretation

HSC 2022 Q24
Task Statement: Use the following Franchise Co. figures to calculate: (1) Current Ratio, (2) Debt-to-Equity Gearing, and (3) Net Profit Ratio. Explain what the Gearing ratio indicates about long-term solvency risk. (6 Marks)
• Current Assets: $80,000 | Current Liabilities: $40,000 | Total Liabilities: $120,000 | Total Equity: $60,000 | Sales: $500,000 | Net Profit: $50,000
Calculations:
1. Current Ratio (CA / CL): ____________________________________________________ Result: _________
2. Debt-to-Equity (TL / OE): __________________________________________________ Result: _________
3. Net Profit Ratio (NP / Sales x 100): __________________________________________ Result: _________
🔑 Band 6 Exemplar & Working

Calculations:
1. Current Ratio: $80,000 / $40,000 = 2:1 (Satisfactory liquidity / short-term capacity)
2. Debt-to-Equity: $120,000 / $60,000 = 2:1 (or 200% gearing ratio)
3. Net Profit Ratio: ($50,000 / $500,000) x 100 = 10% (10c profit per $1 of sales)

Solvency Analysis:
The Debt-to-Equity ratio of 2:1 (200%) indicates Franchise Co. is highly geared, meaning it funds its operations using twice as much debt from external creditors as equity from internal owners. This exposes the business to high financial risk. If interest rates rise, interest expenses will increase, placing pressure on cash flows. While high debt can accelerate growth in boom times, it reduces solvency and increases the risk of insolvency during economic downturns, as debt interest must be paid regardless of sales performance.

Activity 1.2: Working Capital Liquidity Survival

HSC 2018 Q23
Task Statement: Explain how a business can use working capital management strategies to survive a short-term liquidity crisis. (5 Marks)
Marking Rubric:
• 5 Marks: Explains multiple working capital strategies (leasing, sale & leaseback, factoring), showing direct connections to resolving liquidity.
• 3-4 Marks: Explains working capital strategies with some link to liquidity.
• 1-2 Marks: Identifies a financial strategy or defines working capital.
🔑 Band 6 Exemplar

To survive a short-term liquidity crisis (where current liabilities exceed current assets), a business can implement three critical working capital strategies:

1. Accounts Receivable Factoring: The business can sell its outstanding customer invoices to a factoring agency at a small discount (e.g., 95% of value) to receive immediate cash. This turns slow-paying accounts receivable into liquid cash, resolving immediate debt deadlines.

2. Leasing instead of Purchasing: Rather than spending large cash sums purchasing machinery, the business can lease equipment. This keeps cash in the bank for operational expenses, replacing capital outflows with small, regular lease payments.

3. Sale and Leaseback: The business can sell a long-term asset (e.g., its store building) to an investor and lease it back immediately. This injects a large sum of cash into the business to clear current debts while retaining use of the asset.

👷 PRIORITY 2: HUMAN RESOURCES (RIPSE)

Key focus areas: Dispute resolution and quantitative effectiveness indicators.

Activity 2.1: Dispute Resolution Contrast

HSC 2017 Q22
Task Statement: Contrast the roles of mediation and arbitration in resolving workplace disputes. (4 Marks)
🔑 Band 6 Exemplar

Mediation vs. Arbitration Contrast:
Mediation involves a neutral third party who helps disputing parties communicate and negotiate their own mutually acceptable agreement. The mediator has no authority to impose a decision, and any agreement is non-binding.

In contrast, arbitration involves a formal hearing before an independent body (such as the Fair Work Commission) where both sides present their case. The arbitrator reviews the evidence and makes a legally binding decision (an award or order) that both parties must legally follow, regardless of whether they agree with it.

Activity 2.2: Effectiveness Indicators (Turnover & Absenteeism)

HSC 2022 Q21
Task Statement: Explain how staff turnover and absenteeism can indicate the effectiveness of human resource management. (4 Marks)
🔑 Band 6 Exemplar

Staff turnover and absenteeism are quantitative indicators used to measure worker satisfaction and the health of corporate culture:

1. Staff Turnover: Staff turnover refers to the percentage of the workforce that leaves the business and must be replaced each year. High voluntary turnover indicates poor corporate culture, inadequate rewards, or bad leadership styles. High turnover damages HR effectiveness because it raises recruitment and training costs, disrupts scheduling, and reduces productivity.

2. Absenteeism: Absenteeism refers to the rate of employees failing to show up for rosters without notice. High absenteeism is a strong indicator of low morale, worker fatigue, or stressful working conditions. It indicates that HR strategies are ineffective, increases labor costs, and lowers operational consistency.

⚙️ PRIORITY 3: OPERATIONS MANAGEMENT

Key focus areas: The V4 transformation parameters and operations strategies.

Activity 3.1: Variation in Demand (V4 Parameters)

HSC 2021 Q21
Task Statement: Explain how the variation in demand (one of the V4s) influences the transformation processes of a fast-food business. (4 Marks)
🔑 Band 6 Exemplar

Variation in demand refers to how customer volume fluctuates over time, requiring a business to dynamically adjust its inputs and resource allocations. For example, McDonald's experiences extreme variation in demand during daily "lunch rushes" (12:00 PM – 2:00 PM) and seasonal promotions.

To manage this, their transformation processes rely on predictive sequencing software and flexible staff rostering (transforming inputs). Grills and automated drink systems are pre-heated and scaled up during peak hours, whereas inputs are scaled down during off-peak times (e.g., late nights) to minimize food waste and optimize cost efficiency. Failure to predict and manage these variations leads to bottlenecking, long queue times, and lost sales revenue.

Activity 3.2: Operations Strategies (Technology & JIT)

HSC 2020 Q23
Task Statement: Apex Manufactured Goods is experiencing high defect rates on their assembly line and rising inventory holding costs. Write a section of a business report analyzing how two operations strategies (Technology and Inventory Management) could resolve Apex's quality and cost issues. (6 Marks)
🔑 Band 6 Exemplar

Operations Strategies to Address Quality and Cost Concerns:

1. Established & Leading-Edge Technology Upgrades: Apex can resolve high defect rates by incorporating advanced manufacturing technology (computer-aided manufacturing or automated sensors) on the assembly line. By replacing manual inspection with automated precision sensors (leading-edge tech), the business can immediately identify defects in real-time. This reduces waste, improves quality consistency, and lowers long-term operational costs by reducing labor expenses associated with product re-work.

2. Just-In-Time (JIT) Inventory Management: To resolve high holding costs, Apex should transition to a Just-In-Time (JIT) inventory management system. Under JIT, raw materials arrive only as they are needed in the production process. This eliminates storage fees, insurance costs, and the risk of inventory obsolescence, converting idle cash reserves into working capital. However, this strategy requires tight integration with reliable suppliers to prevent production shutdowns if delivery delays occur.

📢 PRIORITY 4: MARKETING (ROLE & INFLUENCES ONLY)

Key focus areas: Consumer laws (Australian Consumer Law) and marketing constraints.

Activity 4.1: Consumer Laws (Implied Conditions & Warranties)

HSC 2016 Q21
Task Statement: Analyse how consumer laws relating to implied conditions or warranties influence a business's marketing activities. (6 Marks)
🔑 Band 6 Exemplar

Consumer laws under the Australian Consumer Law (ACL) place strict legal requirements on how businesses construct and market their goods:

• Implied Conditions: Implied conditions are unspoken, automatic guarantees that a product is fit for purpose, matches descriptions, and is of acceptable quality. If a fast-food business sells cold or unsafe food, it violates an implied condition, which influences operations to enforce strict safety checks to avoid lawsuits and bad press.

• Warranties: A warranty is a promise by a business to repair, replace, or refund defective goods. If a product fails, the business must cover the replacement, which influences finance to allocate warranty reserve cash, and marketing to verify advertising claims do not overpromise product performance.
