* *Total Liabilities:* $120,000 | *Total Equity:* $60,000
* *Sales Revenue:* $500,000 | *Net Profit:* $50,000

**Activity 1: Calculations (Show all working)**
1. **Calculate the Current Ratio:**  
   `Formula: ______________________ | Working: ______________________ | Result: ______`
2. **Calculate the Debt-to-Equity Ratio:**  
   `Formula: ______________________ | Working: ______________________ | Result: ______`
3. **Calculate the Net Profit Ratio (%):**  
   `Formula: ______________________ | Working: ______________________ | Result: ______`

**Activity 2: Interpretation Analysis**
Explain what the calculated **Debt-to-Equity Ratio** indicates about the business's solvency and financial risk. (4 Marks)

---

#### 🔑 Teacher Exemplar Answer (Band 6):
**Calculations:**
1. **Current Ratio:** $80,000 / $40,000 = 2:1 (Satisfactory short-term debt cover)
2. **Debt-to-Equity:** $120,000 / $60,000 = 2:1 (or 200% gearing ratio)
3. **Net Profit Ratio:** ($50,000 / $500,000) x 100 = 10% (For every dollar of sales, 10c is net profit)

**Interpretation (Solvency Analysis):**  
The Debt-to-Equity ratio of 2:1 (200%) indicates that Franchise Co. is highly geared, meaning it funds its operations using twice as much debt from external creditors as equity from internal owners. This exposes the business to high financial risk. 

If interest rates rise, interest expenses will increase, placing pressure on cash flows. While high debt can accelerate growth in boom times, it reduces solvency and increases the risk of insolvency during economic downturns, as debt interest must be paid regardless of sales performance.

#### 🔑 Teacher Exemplar Answer (Band 6):
To survive a short-term liquidity crisis (where current liabilities exceed current assets), a business can implement three critical working capital strategies:
1. **Accounts Receivable Factoring:** The business can sell its outstanding customer invoices to a factoring agency at a small discount (e.g., 95% of value) to receive immediate cash. This turns slow-paying accounts receivable into liquid cash, resolving immediate debt deadlines.
2. **Leasing instead of Purchasing:** Rather than spending large cash sums purchasing machinery, the business can lease equipment. This keeps cash in the bank for operational expenses, replacing capital outflows with small, regular lease payments.
3. **Sale and Leaseback:** The business can sell a long-term asset (e.g., its store building) to an investor and lease it back immediately. This injects a large sum of cash into the business to clear current debts while retaining use of the asset.

#### 🔑 Teacher Exemplar Answer (Band 6):
**Flowchart Steps:**  
1. *Internal Grievance Procedures* (discussion between manager and employee).  
2. *Mediation / Conciliation* (independent third party facilitates, non-binding).  
3. *Arbitration* (Fair Work Commission makes a legally binding decision).

**Mediation vs. Arbitration Contrast:**  
Mediation involves a neutral third party who helps disputing parties communicate and negotiate their own mutually acceptable agreement. The mediator has no authority to impose a decision, and any agreement is non-binding. 

In contrast, arbitration involves a formal hearing before an independent body (such as the Fair Work Commission) where both sides present their case. The arbitrator reviews the evidence and makes a legally binding decision (an award or order) that both parties must legally follow, regardless of whether they agree with it.

---

#### 🔑 Teacher Exemplar Answer (Band 6):
Staff turnover and absenteeism are quantitative indicators used to measure worker satisfaction and the health of corporate culture:

1. **Staff Turnover (Separation rates):**  
   Staff turnover refers to the percentage of the workforce that leaves the business and must be replaced each year. High voluntary turnover indicates poor corporate culture, inadequate rewards, or bad leadership styles. High turnover damages HR effectiveness because it raises recruitment and training costs, disrupts kitchen/factory sequencing, and reduces productivity. Low turnover suggests stable workplace relations.
   
2. **Absenteeism (Unplanned sick days):**  
   Absenteeism refers to the rate of employees failing to show up for rosters without notice. High absenteeism is a strong indicator of low morale, worker fatigue, or stressful working conditions. It indicates that HR strategies (like rosters and workload design) are ineffective. High absenteeism forces managers to find last-minute cover, increases labor costs, and lowers consistency, signaling to management that corporate culture needs intervention.

#### 🔑 Teacher Exemplar Answer (Band 6):
Variation in demand refers to how customer volume fluctuates over time, requiring a business to dynamically adjust its inputs and resource allocations. For example, McDonald’s experiences extreme variation in demand during daily "lunch rushes" (12:00 PM – 2:00 PM) and seasonal promotions. 

To manage this, their transformation processes rely on predictive sequencing software and flexible staff rostering (transforming inputs). Grills and automated drink systems are pre-heated and scaled up during peak hours, whereas inputs are scaled down during off-peak times (e.g., late nights) to minimize food waste and optimize cost efficiency. Failure to predict and manage these variations leads to bottlenecking, long queue times, and lost sales revenue.

---

#### 🔑 Teacher Exemplar Answer (Band 6):
**Operations Strategies to Address Quality and Cost Concerns**

1. **Established & Leading-Edge Technology Upgrades**  
   Apex can resolve high defect rates by incorporating advanced manufacturing technology (computer-aided manufacturing or automated sensors) on the assembly line. By replacing manual inspection with automated precision sensors (leading-edge tech), the business can immediately identify defects in real-time. This reduces waste, improves quality consistency, and lowers long-term operational costs by reducing labor expenses associated with product re-work.
   
2. **Just-In-Time (JIT) Inventory Management**  
   To resolve high holding costs, Apex should transition to a Just-In-Time (JIT) inventory management system. Under JIT, raw materials arrive only as they are needed in the production process. This eliminates storage fees, insurance costs, and the risk of inventory obsolescence, converting idle cash reserves into working capital. However, this strategy requires tight integration with reliable suppliers to prevent production shutdowns if delivery delays occur.

---

#### 🔑 Teacher Exemplar Answer (Band 6):
Consumer laws under the Australian Consumer Law (ACL) place strict legal requirements on how businesses construct and market their goods:

* **Implied Conditions:**  
  Implied conditions are unspoken, automatic guarantees that a product is fit for purpose, matches descriptions, and is of acceptable quality. If a fast-food business sells cold or unsafe food, it violates an implied condition, which influences operations to enforce strict safety checks to avoid lawsuits and bad press.
  
* **Warranties:**  
  A warranty is a promise by a business to repair, replace, or refund defective goods. If a product fails, the business must cover the replacement, which influences finance to allocate warranty reserve cash, and marketing to verify advertising claims do not overpromise product performance.

### Slide 2: Finance Priority — Solvency, Liquidity & Gearing
* **Slide Title:** Balance Sheet Health: Liquidity vs. Solvency (HSC 2022 Q24)
* **Visual Ideas:** A balance scale comparing Current Assets/Liabilities (Liquidity) against Total Debt/Equity (Solvency/Gearing).
* **Bullet Points:**
  * **Liquidity:** Ability to pay short-term debts. Ideal current ratio is 2:1.
  * **Solvency:** Ability to meet long-term commitments.
  * **Gearing:** Ratio of Debt to Equity. High gearing (greater than 100%) = high risk, high reward.
* **Teacher Script:**  
  *"Do not just calculate the ratios. You must interpret them. If gearing is high, tell the examiner that the business has high solvency risk and is vulnerable to interest rate spikes. That is what secures a Band 6."*

### Slide 3: HR Priority — Dispute Resolution Flow
* **Slide Title:** The Path of Workplace Disputes (HSC 2017 Q22)
* **Visual Ideas:** A 3-step staircase arrow representing Grievance ➔ Mediation/Conciliation ➔ Arbitration.
* **Bullet Points:**
  * **Step 1: Grievance:** Internal negotiation, cheap and fast.
  * **Step 2: Mediation:** Third-party helper, non-binding discussion.
  * **Step 3: Arbitration:** Fair Work Commission, legally binding order.
* **Teacher Script:**  
  *"Workplace disputes are highly tested. Remember the distinction: mediation is cooperative and non-binding; arbitration is adversarial, formal, and the resulting decision is legally binding on all parties."*

### Slide 4: Operations Priority — The V4 Transformation Parameters
* **Slide Title:** Managing the V4s in Operations (HSC 2021 Q21)
* **Visual Ideas:** A 2x2 grid representing Volume, Variety, Variation in Demand, and Visibility.
* **Bullet Points:**
  * **Volume:** High volume = standardization (Maccas assembly lines).
  * **Variety:** Low variety = cost leadership. High variety = customisation.
  * **Variation:** Peak vs. off-peak demand (Predictive scheduling).
  * **Visibility:** Customer contact (Open kitchens vs. hidden assembly lines).
* **Teacher Script:**  
  *"Remember, the V4s dictate how a business transforms inputs into outputs. High volume and low variety keep costs down. Variation requires flexible rosters. Make sure you can explain these trade-offs in short-answer questions."*
